# Magnetic field

*Built, in active development, not approved yet* (roadmap O18 step 4,
first slice, 2026-10-27). Implemented in `crates/ohl-electrical`
(`magnetic`). Used by `ohl electrical magnetic` and Checks › Electrical ›
Magnetic field… ([Electrical: magnetic
field](../user-guide/analyses.md#electrical-magnetic-field)).

Every assumption is marked **Assumption** (a value or a model taken where
the line gives none) or **Simplification** (it changes the result), and
said to be **conservative** (the field computed is at least the real one),
**not conservative**, or neither, as far as that can be told. The program
computes the field; **which current, which places and which limit** an
ordinance asks for are the ordinance's (a plugin's parameter file), never
built in.

## What is computed

The **magnetic flux density** of the line's currents at power frequency,
as the rms value of the vector (the *resultant* field):

\\[ B = \sqrt{|\underline B_x|^2 + |\underline B_y|^2 + |\underline B_z|^2} \\]

with \\( \underline B_x, \underline B_y, \underline B_z \\) the complex
rms phasors of the three components. The phases between the components do
not enter (the rms of the vector, not its peak). This is the quantity the
Swiss enforcement guide asks for (BAFU, *Hochspannungsleitungen.
Vollzugshilfe zur NISV*, draft for trial, 2007, section 8.1.4, its
equation 2) and the "resultant field" of IEC 62110 (*Electric and
magnetic field levels generated by AC power systems — Measurement
procedures with regard to public exposure*, 2009).

Beside it the program gives the **largest value over the cycle**: the
field vector \\( \Re(\vec{\underline B}\,e^{j\omega t}) \\) traces an
ellipse; with \\( \vec{\underline B} = \vec R + j\vec I \\) its semi-major
axis (as an rms value) is

\\[ B_{max} = \sqrt{\tfrac12(R^2 + I^2) + \sqrt{\tfrac14(R^2 - I^2)^2 + (\vec R\cdot\vec I)^2}} \\]

(the largest eigenvalue of \\( \vec R\vec R^T + \vec I\vec I^T \\)).
\\( B_{max} \le B \le \sqrt2\,B_{max} \\): equal for a linearly
polarised field, \\( B/\sqrt2 \\) for a circular one.

**Several frequencies** (a 16.7 Hz railway system beside a 50 Hz one):
each frequency computed apart, added as
\\( B = \sqrt{B_{16.7}^2 + B_{50}^2} \\) (Parseval; the guide's equation
3). The largest value over the cycle is then given as the root of the
sum of each frequency's squares — not a physical value (two frequencies
have no common cycle), only an indication.

## The 3D model

Each conductor and earth wire as the solver gives it in the chosen
weather case — the polyline of every span (sag, line angles, the finite
length of the line as modelled), a bundle **subconductor by subconductor**
(each subconductor's polyline offset from the bundle's centre line as the
views draw it, `Bundle::polylines`). Each straight piece from \\( a \\) to
\\( b \\) carries a current \\( \underline I \\) (rms phasor); its field at
\\( p \\) in closed form (Biot–Savart's law integrated along the piece;
e.g. D. J. Griffiths, *Introduction to Electrodynamics*, the field of a
straight wire segment):

\\[ \vec B = \frac{\mu_0 \underline I}{4\pi\rho^2}
    \left( \frac{\vec r_a\cdot\hat u}{|\vec r_a|} - \frac{\vec r_b\cdot\hat u}{|\vec r_b|} \right)
    \hat u \times \vec d \\]

with \\( \hat u \\) the piece's direction, \\( \vec r_a = p - a \\),
\\( \vec r_b = p - b \\), \\( \vec d \\) the perpendicular from the piece's
line to \\( p \\), \\( \rho = |\vec d| \\) — the textbook
\\( \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi\rho}(\cos\alpha_1 - \cos\alpha_2) \\) as a vector.
The pieces' fields are summed per component as phasors.

- **Simplification**: the wire straight between the solver's points. The
  solver says how far the wire lies off its polyline
  (`SpanShape.polyline_error`, centimetres); the field moves by about
  that share of the distance. Neither conservative nor not.
- **Assumption**: a bundle's current shared equally by its
  subconductors (exact for a regular bundle by symmetry, close for the
  others).
- **Assumption**: the permeability of free space everywhere — the steel
  of the structures and of the earth wires' cores is not modelled. It
  changes the field near a structure, little at mid-span.
- **Simplification, not conservative near the structures**: the
  **jumpers are left out** (their currents flow near the structures; at a
  span's lowest point their share is small).
- **The modelled length.** The line is modelled whole, as far as it goes.
  The guide asks for every conductor to be modelled at least five times
  the limit line's largest distance from a conductor before and after the
  plane of calculation (8.1.4.1). The program measures that reach on the
  profile (the farthest point at or above the limit from the nearest
  conductor at the station; without a limit, the profile's half width) and
  **warns** where the line is shorter than five times it on either side —
  near the line's ends, where the lines beyond (a substation, the next
  line) are not modelled: **not conservative** there. The reach is
  measured at the profile's height only; the limit line may reach farther
  at the conductors' height (**not conservative** for the rule's check).

## The 2D model

The wires of a cross-section as **infinite straight parallel
conductors**; per conductor

\\[ \vec B = \frac{\mu_0 \underline I}{2\pi r^2}\,\hat x \times \vec r, \qquad |\vec B| = \frac{\mu_0 |\underline I|}{2\pi r}. \\]

The wires' places: by default **where they cross the profile's plane**
(the cross-section at the station — at the span's lowest point by default:
the wires lowest, the field there largest). For a sagging span this is
**conservative** against the 3D model (the real wires rise towards the
structures; on the synthetic example's 300 m spans the 2D maximum under the lowest point
is 0.1 % above the 3D one, more on long spans with a large sag). Option: the span's **mean height**
(the line parameters' rule) — **not conservative** under the lowest point.

The guide says when 2D is not to be used (8.1.4.2); the program warns:

- **within reach of a line angle** over 5° (a deviation from the straight
  line of more than 5°, the guide's footnote 27): the angle between the
  spans' axes at each structure; a structure within the profile's half
  width (or the limit line's reach, if larger) of the station is said;
- **on a span between differently arranged structures**: a wire's place
  relative to the others (across and up, relative to the wires' centre)
  differs by more than **0.5 m** between the span's two ends (a choice of
  the program, to confirm);
- **near crossings** with other lines: not checked — other lines are not
  in the model yet (said).

## Currents

Per **circuit** (the phases of one system in one named circuit): an rms
current, the same in each phase (**balanced**), **no harmonics**, at the
system's phase angles (L1 0°, L2 −120°, L3 +120° unless the system gives
its own; a transformer's fixed shift, e.g. Yd11, is typed as the system's
angles). This is the governing state the guide describes (2.4.1 and
8.1.4); real operation is unbalanced — **Assumption** as the guide asks,
neither conservative nor not in general.

- **Given** (A), or
- the circuit's **thermal rating** (`--thermal` on the command line): the
  steady-state ampacity of each section's wire ([Thermal
  rating](thermal.md)) times its subconductors, at a conductor
  temperature and in an ambient case **you give** — the least over the
  circuit's sections (the weakest section governs, as the guide's 2.4.1
  says for a line of mixed conductors). The guide's governing current is
  the thermal limit current at the ambient conditions it names (2.4.1);
  those conditions belong to the ordinance's parameter file, not to the
  program.

A **direction** per circuit: the load flow along the line (from its first
structure to its last) or against it — every phase's current turned by
180°. Which combination of directions governs is the ordinance's question
(the guide's 2.4.2); the program computes the one given.

DC systems are left out (a static field; said).

## Earth wire currents

Optional. The earth wires are taken as **infinitely long and earthed
everywhere** (zero voltage along them), so per span, from its mean
cross-section and the primitive series impedances of the [line
parameters](line-parameters.md) (Carson's earth return, the line's
method and soil resistivity):

\\[ \underline I_e = -\,Z_{ee}^{-1} Z_{ep}\,\underline I_p . \\]

**Simplification**: the towers' earthing (footing resistances) and the
earth wires' ends are not modelled; near the ends of a line and with high
footing resistances the real currents are smaller. The guide does not ask
for earth wire currents but takes them when the program computes them
(its footnote 24). Their effect has **either sign**: in the synthetic
double-circuit example (L1 lowest, L3 next to the earth wire) the induced
current is nearly in phase with L1 and **raises** the field near the
ground by about 5 % (one circuit) to 6 % (both); other arrangements lower it. Run both when it
matters.

## The ground's return currents — left out

**Simplification.** The earth's own currents (eddy currents, and the
return of a net current such as an earth wire's) are not modelled: the
field is that of the wires' currents in free space. Their effect can be
estimated with the complex depth (Deri, Tevan, Semlyen, Castanheira,
"The complex ground return plane", *IEEE Trans. PAS-100* (8), 1981): the
earth acts like an image of each current at the depth
\\( h + 2\underline p \\), \\( \underline p = \sqrt{\rho/(j\omega\mu_0)} \\).
At 50 Hz and 100 Ω·m, \\( |\underline p| \approx 503 \\) m: the images lie
about 1 km down.

- **Balanced phases** (the currents sum to zero): the images form a
  balanced set about 1 km away, whose field falls as \\( 1/d^2 \\): for
  1000 A on a flat line of 8 m spacing,
  \\( \sqrt3\,\mu_0 I s/(2\pi d^2) \approx 0.003 \\) µT at the ground.
- **A net current**, e.g. 100 A in an earth wire returning through the
  earth: \\( \mu_0 I/(2\pi d) \approx 2\cdot10^{-7}\cdot 100/1000 \approx 0.02 \\) µT.
- At 10 Ω·m (\\( |\underline p| \approx 160 \\) m) about three times
  that.

Small against fields of the order of 1 µT; larger in relative terms far
from the line, where the field is small anyway (the guide notes the
model's uncertainty grows well below 1 µT, 8.1.4). Neither conservative
nor not in general (the images oppose their sources' near fields).

## Where

- **Profiles** across the line at stations (every span's lowest point by
  default — the lowest point of its lowest phase conductor — or a place
  in a span), square to the span's axis, ±50 m every 1 m, at a height
  above the ground (1 m by default). The ground: the terrain surface when
  there is one, else straight between the structures' feet and level
  across (**Simplification**).
- **Grids** on a horizontal plane at that height over a span (for
  isolines; drawing them is not yet built).
- **Given points** (e.g. places of sensitive use) from a CSV file: x, y and
  an elevation or a height above the ground.

With an optional **limit** (µT, yours to give): the margin at each point
(limit − B) and per profile the extent where B is at or above it (the
limit line's width at that height), and whether it reaches beyond the
profile.

## Validation

`benchmarks/electrical/magnetic.toml` (see [Textbook and published
cases](../validation/textbook.md#magnetic-field-benchmarkselectricalmagnetictoml)):
one infinite conductor (\\( \mu_0 I/(2\pi d) \\) = 20 µT, 2D and as a
200 km segment), finite segments and a sagging wire beside a second phase
against Biot–Savart's law integrated numerically (`reference_magnetic.py`,
scipy's adaptive quadrature; the ellipse by sampling the cycle) within
\\( 10^{-9} \\)–\\( 10^{-8} \\), a balanced flat line near it, and far
away against its closed form \\( \sqrt3\mu_0 I s/(2\pi d^2) \\) and its
\\( 1/d^2 \\) decay. Tests in the crate: the field of a split segment, a
reversed one, the ellipse's axes, and on the synthetic double circuit the
symmetry, the load-flow directions and phase arrangement, the earth wire's
currents, 2D against 3D, the 2D line angle warning, given points.

**Not validated yet** against a published profile with all its inputs (to
find: IEC 62110's examples, CIGRE technical brochures on fields of
overhead lines, papers comparing with measurements); comparisons with
other programs on real lines stay with the owner.
