# Member capacities by SIA 263:2013

Implemented in `crates/ohl-structures-fe/src/sia263.rs` (`capacity`,
`Stability::usage`, `chi`, `chi_lt`, `rho`, `eta`, `omega`,
`bolt_minimums`, `standard_hole`), used by the member check
(`crates/ohl-structures-fe/src/check.rs`) when a model's `[analysis]
capacity = "sia263-2013"`, and by the bolt tools
(`ohl_structures_fe::bolt_rules`). Built, in active development (not
approved yet); pre-alpha: results must be verified by a qualified
engineer.

**Source.** SIA 263:2013, *Construction en acier* (Swiss Society of
Engineers and Architects), the Swiss steel standard based on Eurocode 3.
Clause, table, figure and formula numbers below are the standard's. It is
a capacity method beside [ASCE 10-97](asce10.md), which stays the
default.

## Design values

The capacities are **design values** \\( R_d \\) with the resistance
factors of 4.1.3: \\( \gamma_{M1} = 1.05 \\) (members) and
\\( \gamma_{M2} = 1.25 \\) (net sections, connections). The load case's
strength factor \\( \phi \\) still multiplies them as for every method, so
load cases for SIA set \\( \phi = 1 \\) and factor the actions (SIA 260).

## Section class

Each compressed element's \\( b/t \\) against Tables 5a (supported at both
edges: 33, 38, 42 \\( \varepsilon \\)) and 5b (outstands: 9, 10, 14
\\( \varepsilon \\)), tubes' \\( D/t \\) against 50, 70, 90
\\( \varepsilon^2 \\); \\( \varepsilon = \sqrt{235/f_y} \\) (\\( f_y \\) in
MPa). The section takes its worst element's class.

| Profile | Elements (for the class) |
|---|---|
| angle (and the angles of built-ups) | the larger leg \\( h/t \\), outstand |
| I section | half flange \\( (b/2)/t_f \\), outstand; web \\( (h - 2t_f)/t_w \\), internal |
| channel | flange \\( b/t_f \\), outstand; web as an I section's |
| rectangular hollow | larger wall less \\( 1.5t \\) at each side, \\( (h - 3t)/t \\), internal (Table 5a) |
| polygonal hollow | the flat at mid-thickness \\( (D - t)\tan(\pi/n) \\) over \\( t \\), internal |
| circular hollow | \\( D/t \\), tube |

**Conservative:** the class of uniform compression is used for every
check, also for bending (where webs have higher limits); an angle's leg
and an I section's half flange are measured whole (Table 5b does not draw
where an angle's \\( b \\) starts).

**Class 4** (above class 3): effective widths \\( b_{eff} = \rho\,b \\)
(4.5.3.2) with \\( \rho = 0.9/\bar\lambda_P \le 1 \\),
\\( \bar\lambda_P = (b/t)/(28.4\,\varepsilon\sqrt{k}) \\) (4.5.3.3), \\( k \\)
from Table 9 for uniform compression: 0.426 for an outstand, 4.0 for an
internal element; \\( b \\) here from the theoretical lines (4.5.3.3): an
angle's leg \\( h - t/2 \\), an I section's half flange \\( b/2 \\), a web
\\( h - t_f \\), a hollow section's wall \\( h - t \\). The effective area
\\( A_{eff} = A - \sum (1 - \rho)\,b\,t \\). A class 4 tube has no rule in
SIA 263 (shell buckling): the member is said to be outside the standard
and gets no capacity.

**Simplification:** a class 4 section's moduli \\( W_{eff} \\) are taken
as \\( \rho_{min} W_{el} \\) (the least \\( \rho \\) of its plates; an angle's
from its effective section, below) — on the safe side for sections whose
every plate is reduced, approximate otherwise. 4.5.3.8 (\\( f_y \\) by
\\( \sqrt{\chi} \\) for the effective width at a buckling stress) is not
used: on the safe side.

## Flexural buckling

4.5.1.3, formula (7): \\( N_{K,Rd} = \chi_K f_y A / \gamma_{M1} \\) with
(8):

\\[ \Phi_K = 0.5\left[1 + \alpha_K(\bar\lambda_K - 0.2) + \bar\lambda_K^2\right], \qquad \chi_K = \frac{1}{\Phi_K + \sqrt{\Phi_K^2 - \bar\lambda_K^2}} \le 1 \\]

\\( \bar\lambda_K = \lambda_K/\lambda_E \\), \\( \lambda_K = L_K/i \\),
\\( \lambda_E = \pi\sqrt{E/f_y} \\); \\( \alpha_K \\) of Table 8: a 0.21, b
0.34, c 0.49, d 0.76 (`chi`). Class 4: \\( A_{eff} \\) instead of \\( A \\)
and \\( \bar\lambda_K = \sqrt{A_{eff}/A}\,\sqrt{f_y/\sigma_{cr,K}} \\) (5.3.8).

**Curves** (Figure 7): angles, channels, T sections, built-ups of angles
and solid bars c; I sections b about the strong axis and c about the weak
one up to \\( t_f \\) = 40 mm, c and d beyond; hollow sections c; a
`general` profile d.

**Conservative:** rolled and welded I sections are not told apart (the
welded ones' curves; a rolled section with \\( (h - t_f)/b > 1.2 \\) would
take a and b); hollow sections as cold-formed (c; hot-finished ones would
take a); a profile not known d.

**Axes and lengths.** An angle (single or crossed) buckles about its
principal axes u and v, other sections about y and z (and a minor
principal axis v when it differs). \\( L_K = L \\) between the joints held
(5.4.1.3): the member's length ratios over y, z, v, else 1 (said); the
major axis u of an angle takes the larger of the y and z ratios. **A
single angle connected eccentrically and crossed angles** buckle about
the minor axis with \\( L_K = 0.9\,L \\) (5.4.1.6). The reduced lengths of
Table 13 (5.4.1.4, 5.4.1.5) apply only where the joints restrain the
members enough: the program does not judge that; they are given as the
member's length ratios.

A crossing diagonal whose partner is not in tension (or has no partner)
is taken unbraced out of its plane over its whole length about its
smaller geometric axis, as for ASCE 10-97.

**Torsional and torsional-flexural buckling.** 4.5.1.2 says they may
govern "in special cases" without giving a method. The program takes
them by elastic theory, as in the [ASCE 10-97 page](asce10.md) (\\( r_t \\),
\\( r_{tf} \\) by the profile's symmetry), as a slenderness on the weaker
axis's curve; single angles and closed sections are not concerned.

**Slenderness limits** (4.5.1.7): \\( \lambda_K \\) at most 200 for main
load-carrying members (legs, bracing, crossing diagonals, members without
a class) and 250 for bracing of other members and secondary members (class
`redundant`); above it is said with the usage. The limit of 160 for
members under fatigue is not checked.

## Angles bolted by one leg: the eccentricity

5.4.1.2 requires the bending from the connections' eccentricity in the
member's check; 5.4.1.6 sets the minor-axis buckling length. The program
applies **both** (owner: the literal reading):

- the force enters on the back of the bolted leg at its gauge line: the
  mean of the connection's gauge lines; without them the farthest gauge
  Table 17 allows (the leg less the minimum edge distance \\( e_2 \\);
  **conservative**), without a bolt of a listed size the middle of the
  leg's flat (said);
- its offset from the centroid \\( (\Delta y, \Delta z) \\) is turned into
  the principal axes: \\( e_u = \Delta y\cos\theta + \Delta z\sin\theta \\),
  \\( e_v = -\Delta y\sin\theta + \Delta z\cos\theta \\); \\( N e_v \\) bends
  about u, \\( N e_u \\) about v, uniformly along the member
  (\\( \omega = 1 \\));
- classes 1–3: formula (50), 5.1.10.1 (5.2.9 for class 3 with elastic
  resistances):

\\[ \frac{N_{Ed}}{N_{K,Rd}} + \frac{\omega_u}{1 - N_{Ed}/N_{cr,u}}\frac{M_{u,Ed}}{M_{D,Rd}} + \frac{\omega_v}{1 - N_{Ed}/N_{cr,v}}\frac{M_{v,Ed}}{M_{v,Rd}} \le 1 \\]

  with \\( N_{cr} = \pi^2 E I/L_K^2 \\) per axis, \\( M_{Rd} = f_y W_{el}/\gamma_{M1} \\)
  (**conservative** for classes 1 and 2: the elastic modulus, angles' plastic
  moduli about their principal axes not being in the section library),
  \\( M_{D,Rd} \\) by lateral-torsional buckling (below);
- class 4: the stress checks (62) and (63) of 5.3.9, each with one axis
  amplified:

\\[ \sigma_{Ed} = \frac{N_{Ed}}{A_{eff}} + \frac{1}{1 - N_{Ed}/N_{cr,u}}\frac{N_{Ed}(e_u' + w_{0,u})}{W_{u,eff}} + \frac{N_{Ed}(e_v' + w_{0,v})}{W_{v,eff}} \le \frac{f_y}{\gamma_{M1}} \\]

  (and the same with v amplified), \\( e' \\) the connection's eccentricity
  plus the shift of the effective section's centroid (5.3.5; the angle's
  legs reduced to \\( t/2 + \rho(h - t/2) \\) from the heel), \\( w_0 \\) the
  bow of Figure 3 for the elastic method by the curve (a \\( L/300 \\), b
  \\( L/250 \\), c \\( L/200 \\), d \\( L/150 \\)), \\( L \\) the bar between
  its holds.

The usage is the larger of this and the axial check (the connection's
bolts may govern). In tension the eccentricity is taken by 6.2.4.4
(below), the norm's own rule for an angle bolted by one leg, not as a
bending moment: **an interpretation** (5.4.1.2 does not say whether it
means compression only).

**Built-up angles** (two angles back to back, stars, crosses) are taken
as connected concentrically: **not on the safe side** where a gusset
between two angles is off their centroid in the other direction.

## Frames: bending with axial force

A frame member (not a lattice member) under compression and bending:
formula (50) with its end moments about y and z, \\( \omega = 0.6 + 0.4\psi
\ge 0.4 \\) (5.1.9.1, Figure 16; \\( \psi \\) the smaller end moment over
the larger, with signs), \\( M_{Rd} = f_y W/\gamma_{M1} \\) (\\( W_{pl} \\)
for classes 1 and 2 where the profile gives it, \\( W_{el} \\) for 3,
\\( W_{eff} \\) for 4 by (62)/(63)). In tension: \\( N/N_{Rd} + M_y/M_{y,Rd}
+ M_z/M_{z,Rd} \le 1 \\) (the elastic interaction of 5.2; **conservative**
for classes 1 and 2 against 5.1.6).

**Conservative:** the end moments come from the second-order solve
(P-Delta, the default) and (50) amplifies them again by
\\( 1/(1 - N/N_{cr}) \\): the formula is written for first-order moments.
The member's bow within its length is in (50) through \\( N_{K,Rd} \\).

## Lateral-torsional buckling

4.5.2.2, formula (9): \\( M_{D,Rd} = \chi_D W f_y/\gamma_{M1} \\),
\\( \chi_D \\) by (10) with \\( \bar\lambda_D = \sqrt{W f_y/M_{cr}} \\) and
\\( \Phi_D = 0.5[1 + \alpha_D(\bar\lambda_D - 0.4) + \bar\lambda_D^2] \\)
(`chi_lt`). \\( M_{cr} = W_{el}\sigma_{cr,D} \\) by Annex B: \\( \sigma_{cr,D}
= \sqrt{\sigma_{Dv}^2 + \sigma_{Dw}^2} \\) (91), \\( \sigma_{Dv} = \eta
\frac{\pi}{L_D W_{el}}\sqrt{GKEI_z} \\) (92), \\( \sigma_{Dw} = \pi^2 E/\lambda_K^2 \\)
of the compressed chord (the flange and a third of the compressed half
web, Figure 40) with \\( L_K = L_D/\sqrt\eta \\) (93); \\( \eta = 1.75 -
1.05\psi + 0.3\psi^2 \\) (94; below \\( \psi = -0.5 \\) its value there,
**conservative**). \\( L_D \\): the weak axis's buckling length (an angle:
its minor axis's, without the 0.9).

**Conservative:** \\( \alpha_D \\) = 0.49 (welded; rolled sections would
take 0.21); Table 6's lengths below which no check is needed are not
used (\\( \chi_D \\) is 1 up to \\( \bar\lambda_D \\) = 0.4 anyway).

**Simplification:** Annex B is for doubly symmetric sections. Channels
and angles take \\( \sigma_{Dv} \\) alone (no chord term): approximate,
either way (their shear centre is off the centroid). Closed sections and
bars: no lateral-torsional buckling.

## Tension

The least of, at each end (the weaker end governs, named):

- the gross section \\( f_y A/\gamma_{M1} \\) (38);
- the net section \\( 0.9 f_u A_{net}/\gamma_{M2} \\) (39), \\( A_{net} = A -
  n\,d_0\,t \\) with the holes of one section \\( n \\) (from the gauge
  lines, else 1, said) and the hole \\( d_0 \\) (given, else Table 17's for
  the bolt; another size: the bolt + 3 mm, said);
- an angle bolted by one leg (6.2.4.4, Figure 30): one bolt (84)
  \\( 0.9 f_u (2e_2 - d_0) t/\gamma_{M2} \\); several on a line (83)
  \\( (0.9 f_u A_t + f_y A_{v}/\sqrt3)/\gamma_{M2} \\), \\( A_t = (e_2 -
  d_0/2)t \\), \\( A_v = ((n-1)p + e_1)t \\), \\( e_2 \\) from the line to the
  toe (the connection's gauge, else the edge distance). Several gauge
  lines (6.2.4.6 asks for "particular attention"): the outer line alone
  carries the force (**conservative**, said);
- the bolts' shear and bearing (below).

## Bolts

Table 16, per shear plane, the threads in the plane:
\\( F_{v,Rd} = 0.6 f_{ub} A_s/\gamma_{M2} \\) for grades 4.6, 5.6 and 8.8 (73),
\\( 0.5 f_{ub} A_s/\gamma_{M2} \\) for 4.8, 5.8, 6.8 and 10.9 (74); the
threads out of it \\( 0.6 f_{ub} A/\gamma_{M2} \\) (75). A library bolt's own
shear capacity is taken as characteristic and divided by
\\( \gamma_{M2} \\).

**Conservative:** a grade not given takes 0.5; the tensile stress area
\\( A_s \\) is taken as 0.6 of the gross area when the bolt gives no
capacity (as for ASCE 10-97; \\( A_s \\) is larger for usual bolts); the
threads in the shear plane unless said.

Bearing (76): \\( F_{b,Rd} = 0.85\frac{e_1}{d_0}\frac{f_u}{\gamma_{M2}} d t \le
2.4\frac{f_u}{\gamma_{M2}} d t \\) per bolt, \\( f_u \\) of the member, times
the bolts and plies; with several bolts on a line and \\( p_1 < e_1 +
d_0/2 \\), \\( e_1 \\) is replaced by \\( p_1 - d_0/2 \\) for all of them
(**conservative**: Table 16 says it for the inner bolts). Without an end
distance, Table 17's minimum (said). An edge distance \\( e_2 < d_0 \\) is
outside the formula's conditions and said with the usage.

**Minimum distances** (6.2.6, Table 17) for the bolt tools: end distance
\\( e_1 \\), spacing \\( p_1 \\) and edge distance \\( e_2 \\) by the bolt's
size (M10 to M30; another size has no rule). Table 17's footnote that
\\( p \\) below the usual spacing needs the bearing check is the check
above.

## Steel pole shafts

A pole (`.ohlp`, or `[pole]`) of steel, hollow, with its `yield` and no
capacity given, and `code = "sia263-2013"` (2026-11-03; the default stays
[ASCE/SEI 48-19](asce48.md)): its shaft is checked at the pole check's
heights with the forces of that page (`sia263::shaft_usage`):

- the class by Table 5a: a round tube (and a polygon of more than 16
  sides) by \\( D/t \\) against 50, 70, 90 \\( \varepsilon^2 \\) — a class 4
  tube has no rule in SIA 263 (shell buckling): said, usage infinite; a
  polygon by its flats at mid-thickness \\( (D - t)\tan(\pi/n) \\) as
  internal elements (33, 38, 42 \\( \varepsilon \\)), a class 4 one by its
  effective flats (\\( \rho \\), \\( k = 4 \\): \\( A_{eff} \\), \\( W_{eff} \approx
  \rho W \\), **conservative**);
- \\( \sigma = N/A + M/W \\) (elastic, 5.2; **conservative** for classes 1
  and 2), \\( \tau = 2V/A + T/(2A_m t) \\) as on the ASCE/SEI 48 page;
- the von Mises criterion of 3.2.2.5, \\( \sqrt{\sigma^2 + 3\tau^2} \le
  f_y/\gamma_{M1} \\), on the compression and the tension side.

**Not on the safe side:** the pole's buckling as a member (4.5.1, formula
49) is not checked; the moments carry the P-Delta amplification when the
pole gives its modulus, but without the imperfections of 4.2.3 (4.5.1.1
allows a second-order analysis only with them). A slender pole under a
large vertical load can be unsafe.

## Plates and welds

The gusset plates are checked per case with the analysis'
member forces ([Gusset plates and their welds](joint-plates.md)): per
member bolting onto a plate its bearing on the plate (Table 16, (76)), the
plate's net and block shear (6.2.4.3, (82), (83)), its gross and net
sections (5.1.2.1, (38), (39)) and the distances of Table 17; the fillet
welds between plates by 6.3.2.2, (87) on the throat \\( a \\) (the weld's
size) and (88) on the contact width, under the resultant they carry
spread evenly along them (6.3.1.7). A full penetration weld is not
checked (6.3.3.4, 6.3.1.8). A plate in compression: plate buckling not
checked (no rule for gusset plates in the standard).

## Not covered

Unsafe where one of them governs; the standard's clause is the reference.

- shear of members (5.1.4) and its interaction (5.1.7, 5.3.6); torsion;
- class 4 tubes; cold-formed sections (5.7); built-up members' battens
  (5.5);
- slip-resistant connections (6.2.3), bolts in tension (6.2.2.3–6.2.2.6),
  long joints (6.2.2.2), welds (6.3);
- fatigue (4.7), fire (4.8), earthquake (4.9), serviceability (4.10);
- the yield strength by thickness (Table 1: one value per section).

## Validation

Unit tests (`sia263::tests`, `sia263_tests`): the curves at their
printed points (χ at \\( \bar\lambda \\) 0.2, \\( \lambda_E \\) of S 235 in
Figure 7, \\( \varepsilon \\) of Table 5b, \\( \eta \\) and \\( \omega \\) at
Figures 16 and 41), Table 10's limits of \\( \rho \\), Tables 5a/5b's
classes, Table 17; an L 60×6 bolted by one leg by hand (\\( N_{K,Rd} \\)
about v with 0.9 L, the bolt's shear and bearing, the tension by (84),
the gauge, formula (50)); an L 100×6 of class 4 by (62)/(63) by hand; the
method's name and the bolt rules.
