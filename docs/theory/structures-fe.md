# Structure analysis: frames, trusses, guys and poles

Implemented in `crates/ohl-structures-fe`: a linear-elastic 3D finite-element model of a structure (lattice tower, frame, guyed mast, pole) under static loads, first order or with geometric non-linearity (P-Delta), and the member and pole checks built on it. It is a *base*: the formulation is standard matrix structural analysis; design-code specifics (column curves, connection checks, wind on the structure) are left to the data or to later work.

## Model and axes

Nodes are given in the **structure's axes**: x to the right of the line looking ahead, y ahead, z up, origin at the ground centre (the axes of the structure types, `.ohls`). Each node has six degrees of freedom \\( (u_x, u_y, u_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z) \\); rotations exist only at nodes with a frame member, fixed degrees of freedom are removed, springs add to the diagonal.

Each member has **local axes**: \\( x \\) from node *i* to node *j*; a reference direction \\( \mathbf r \\) in the local x–y plane (default: global z, or global x for a vertical member) gives \\( \mathbf z = \mathbf x \times \mathbf r / |\cdot| \\), \\( \mathbf y = \mathbf z \times \mathbf x \\); a roll angle turns y and z about x. \\( I_z \\) is the bending in the local x–y plane, \\( I_y \\) in the x–z plane. An angle's legs given as two structure directions \\( \mathbf a, \mathbf b \\) (`orientation = "x+y-"`) set \\( \mathbf r = \mathbf a \\) when \\( \hat{\mathbf x}\cdot(\mathbf a_\perp \times \mathbf b_\perp) > 0 \\) (\\( \mathbf a_\perp \\): the part normal to the member), so that the section's y leg points to \\( \mathbf a \\) and its z leg to the side of \\( \mathbf b \\); otherwise the pair is the mirror image of a right-handed section: \\( \mathbf r = \mathbf b \\) for a section with equal legs, refused for unequal ones. The element matrices are transformed with \\( \mathbf T = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf R, \mathbf R, \mathbf R, \mathbf R) \\), \\( \mathbf K = \mathbf T^T \mathbf k \mathbf T \\).

## Elements

- **Truss** (axial only): \\( \mathbf k = \frac{EA}{L}\\,\mathbf e\mathbf e^T \\) in the 3×3 blocks \\( \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf k & -\mathbf k \\\\ -\mathbf k & \mathbf k \end{bmatrix} \\).
- **Cable** (guys): a truss in tension only, with an initial force \\( T_0 \\) (pretension): \\( N = T_0 + \frac{EA}{L}\\,\mathbf e\cdot(\mathbf u_j - \mathbf u_i) \\). The pretension acts as a pair of nodal forces pulling the ends together; the force after the structure deflects differs from \\( T_0 \\) by the structure's give. A cable whose force would fall below zero is **slack**: it is removed and the solve repeated until the set of slack cables does not change.
- **Tension-only truss** (`tension`): a truss in tension and in compression up to its own elastic buckling load \\( N_{cr} \\); beyond it **bowed** — left out of the stiffness, carrying \\( N_{cr} \\) — and with \\( N_{cr} = 0 \\) (no second moment) slack, by the active-set iterations below. No pretension.
- **Frame**: the 12×12 elastic stiffness of the 3D Euler–Bernoulli beam (axial \\( EA/L \\), torsion \\( GJ/L \\), bending \\( 12EI/L^3, 6EI/L^2, 4EI/L, 2EI/L \\) in both planes) — Przemieniecki, *Theory of Matrix Structural Analysis* (1968), ch. 5. Shear deformation is neglected.

**Self-weight**: \\( w = f_d\\,\rho A g \\) per metre — \\( f_d \\) the member's dead-load factor (its own, else the model's `dead_load_factor`, default 1: bolts, plates) — times the load case's vertical factor. On frames as consistent nodal loads (\\( wL/2 \\) and \\( wL^2/12 \\) end moments, in local axes), on trusses and cables half at each end. The reported member end forces of frames subtract the fixed-end part, so they are the true end forces.

## Second order (P-Delta)

With `nonlinear = true` (the default), the tangent stiffness includes the **geometric stiffness** of each member's axial force \\( N \\) (tension positive):

- truss and cable: \\( \frac{N}{L}(\mathbf I - \mathbf e\mathbf e^T) \\) in the 3×3 blocks;
- frame: the consistent geometric stiffness of the cubic beam (terms \\( \frac{N}{L}\\{\tfrac65, \tfrac{L}{10}, \tfrac{2L^2}{15}, -\tfrac{L^2}{30}\\} \\) in both bending planes) — McGuire, Gallagher & Ziemian, *Matrix Structural Analysis* (2nd ed., 2000), ch. 9. The torsion–bending coupling terms are left out.

This captures both P-Δ (sway of the member chords) and P-δ (bowing within a frame member, refined by more elements). Displacements remain small (no corotational update of the geometry); the analysis is a *second-order elastic* analysis in the sense of McGuire et al., ch. 9.

**Newton iterations**: the out-of-balance forces \\( \mathbf r = \mathbf F - \mathbf K_T(N)\\,\mathbf u \\) are solved with the tangent \\( \mathbf K_T = \mathbf K_e + \mathbf K_g(N) \\) (the change of \\( N \\) with \\( \mathbf u \\) is not in the tangent), \\( \mathbf u \mathrel{+}= \mathbf K_T^{-1}\mathbf r \\), the axial forces and slack cables updated, until \\( |\mathbf r| \le 10^{-9} \\) of the larger of \\( |\mathbf F| \\) and the largest pretension, and the slack set is stable. Where the axial forces are statically determinate (a cantilever column) the second iteration is final; otherwise the iterations converge linearly with the ratio of the loads to the buckling load. A negative pivot in the factorisation means the tangent stiffness is no longer positive definite: the loads are above an **elastic buckling** load, reported as such.

**Exit clauses** (`ohl_core::budget`): 200 iterations and 30 s by default; when one stops the solve, the error says which and how large the out-of-balance forces still were.

## Solver

The stiffness is symmetric and banded once the nodes are numbered well: nodes are ordered by **reverse Cuthill–McKee** (George & Liu, *Computer Solution of Large Sparse Positive Definite Systems*, 1981) and the matrix is stored as a **skyline** (profile) and factorised \\( \mathbf L\mathbf D\mathbf L^T \\) in place (active-column method, Bathe, *Finite Element Procedures*, §8.2). The cost is about \\( n\bar b^2/2 \\) for \\( n \\) equations of mean column height \\( \bar b \\): a generated 1000-member lattice tower solves in a few milliseconds (release build). A zero pivot names the node and direction that are free to move (a mechanism).

Reactions are reported at every joint with a support or a spring: in each of its six directions, the forces of its members on it less its loads, \\( R = \mathbf f_{int} - \mathbf f_{ext} \\). At a fixed direction that is the support's reaction. A sprung direction moves, in balance with its spring, \\( \mathbf f_{int} + k u = \mathbf f_{ext} \\), so the same expression is the spring's force on the joint, \\( -k u \\) — translations (N/m) and rotations (N·m/rad) alike, first order and with P-Delta (there to the solve's residual). A direction both fixed and sprung does not move: the support takes everything and the spring nothing. So \\( \sum R + \sum \text{loads} = 0 \\) (the self-weight included) whether the structure stands on supports, springs or both; a joint held by springs only (a foundation on springs) has its reaction too. Benchmarks `structfe-009` (a beam on a vertical spring: the spring's force \\( Pa/L \\) by statics, its travel that over \\( k \\)) and `structfe-010` (a cantilever on a rotational spring: the base moment \\( PL \\), the tip \\( PL^3/3EI + PL^2/k_r \\)).

A **mechanism** is reported with the part that moves: after the zero pivot, the stiffness is assembled again and factorised with its zero pivots replaced by \\( 10^{-12} \\) of its largest diagonal entry; two steps of inverse iteration from a fixed start give a vector dominated by the zero-energy motions (they grow by the ratio of the stiffness to that stand-in, the others do not), and the joints translating more than 1/1000 of the largest translation (rotating, when none translates) are named.

## Joint stabilisation

Towers modelled as trusses have joints with no stiffness of their own in some direction; the stiffness matrix is then singular. Following the practice of tower programs, which stabilise such joints, the solver holds those directions — only when a solve without them fails (a zero pivot, or a negative one: buckling), so that a model that solves as it is is solved by exactly the same operations and its results are unchanged bit for bit. The directions come from the geometry (`ohl_structures_fe::stabilise`):

- **Translations** of a joint without a frame member: the eigenvectors of \\( \mathbf S = \sum \mathbf e\mathbf e^T \\) over the unit directions \\( \mathbf e \\) of its truss and cable members and of its supported or sprung axes, with an eigenvalue below \\( 10^{-6} \\) of the largest (\\( \mathbf e^T\mathbf n \\) is the sine of a member's angle to the free plane, so this is about 0.06°): the normal of a plane of coplanar members, the plane normal to collinear ones. The joint's elastic stiffness along such an \\( \mathbf n \\) is \\( \sum \frac{EA}{L} (\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf n)^2 \approx 0 \\).
- **Rotations** about the common axis \\( \mathbf a \\) of a chain of collinear frame members, when no joint of the chain has a frame in another direction or a rotational support or spring with a component about \\( \mathbf a \\): a rigid rotation of the chain about its axis strains nothing (no twist between the ends, no bending, no translation). One joint of the chain (its first in node order) is held. Joints with truss members only have no rotational degrees of freedom at all.

A free direction along a structure axis (within \\( 1 - \cos < 10^{-9} \\)) is held **exactly**: its degree of freedom is removed, as a support would. A skew one gets a spring \\( k\\,\mathbf n\mathbf n^T \\) with \\( k \\) the largest \\( EA/L \\) (rotations: \\( GJ/L \\)) at the joint — for several free directions the sum is the projector onto the free space, whatever basis the eigen-solver returns. In first-order analysis nothing else acts along a free direction (the members' forces are normal to it), so the restraint's force is exactly the load component along it and the rest of the solution is that of the structure with the direction fixed; with P-Delta the geometric stiffness of tension members across the direction (\\( N/L \\)) couples it to its neighbours, and the restraint keeps it from moving, as a tower program's stabilisation does. The force each stabilisation takes (the joint's out-of-balance along its direction) is reported, and subtracted from the reaction of a supported joint so that reactions are the supports' own. A stabilisation that takes more than 1 % of the loads (the larger of their resultant and the largest load at a joint; a moment over the model's size) and more than a quarter of its joint's own load is refused as a mechanism: a load across a plane no member holds is not carried by the structure. Below that — the share of the self-weight across a battered face, sin(batter) of the joint's load — it is reported as a note.

What is not a single joint's freedom (a rigid motion of a part of several joints: a missing support, a hinge chain) is not found by these rules and remains a zero pivot after the stabilisation: reported as a mechanism with the joints that move.

## Tension-only members: compression up to their buckling load

A tension-only member (`kind = "tension"`) stands for light bracing that buckles under a small compression. It follows a **capped law**: a truss in tension and in compression up to its own elastic buckling load, and no more:

\\[ N = \max\left(\frac{EA}{L}\\,\delta,\\; -N_{cr}\right), \qquad N_{cr} = \frac{\pi^2 E I_{min}}{L_k^2}, \\]

\\( \delta \\) its stretch, \\( L_k \\) its `lk`, else its `k` × its length — \\( k = 1 \\) by default: **pinned ends**, the member's own length between its joints (as for the compression check below). Shortened beyond \\( N_{cr} \\), the member is **bowed**: it carries \\( N_{cr} \\) and the further shortening costs nothing (an elastic strut after buckling keeps about its Euler load; the post-buckling rise and the bow's bending are left out). A member without a second moment (\\( I_{min} = 0 \\)) has \\( N_{cr} = 0 \\): **slack** in compression, as a rope — the earlier tension-only behaviour exactly. Not a sagging cable: a bowed member has no sag, no weight-dependent stiffness. This is the owner's decision of 2026-10-19 (ROADMAP, O5): it always solves, close to reality, where a member left out entirely could leave a part nearly free.

The law is monotone (non-decreasing in \\( \delta \\)) and its energy convex — the elastic–perfectly-plastic law in compression, without unloading history. Solved as tower programs solve tension-only members, by a sequence of linear (or P-Delta) solves on a changing set of **active** members: a member out of the set (bowed, or slack) has no stiffness and carries the constant \\( N_{cr} \\) — a pair of forces pushing its ends apart, put with the loads like a cable's pretension, reported as its axial force. With \\( \mathcal S \\) the set left out, starting empty:

1. solve the model without the stiffness of the members of \\( \mathcal S \\), each carrying \\( -N_{cr} \\) (stabilised as above on the model of the active members);
2. \\( \mathcal S' = \\{ m \notin \mathcal S : N_m < -N_{cr,m} - \varepsilon \\} \cup \\{ m \in \mathcal S : \frac{EA}{L}\\,\delta_m \le -N_{cr,m} + \varepsilon \\} \\), with \\( \delta_m \\) the stretch of a left-out member (below) and \\( \varepsilon = 10^{-9} \max |N| \\) of the solve, so that round-off on a member at its load (or without force) does not switch it;
3. stop when \\( \mathcal S' = \mathcal S \\), else \\( \mathcal S \leftarrow \mathcal S' \\) and repeat.

A member compressed less than its \\( N_{cr} \\) stays active: a truss (a stocky tension-only member solves exactly as a truss). Tension-only members meeting at a joint on one line (within 0.06°) are switched as **one tie** — a diagonal in two halves through its crossing with the other is one rope: a half alone would hang from the crossing — by its stretch end to end over its flexibility, \\( \sum_m \delta_m / \sum_m \frac{L_m}{EA_m} \\) (\\( \delta_m = N_m L_m/EA_m \\) for an active member), in place of \\( N_m \\) and \\( \frac{EA}{L}\delta_m \\) above, against the least \\( N_{cr} \\) of its members (the force along the tie is one; at an unloaded crossing the bowed halves' forces then balance). All ties are switched at once. With **P-Delta** the set is found by first-order solves and then refined by P-Delta solves started from it, each converged (Newton) before the set is updated — outside the Newton loop: a compressed tension-only member's negative geometric stiffness \\( N/L \\) in the first solves is not real and could make the tangent indefinite, and an update inside the loop would change the matrix between iterations of one solution. A bowed member keeps the geometric stiffness of its constant force, \\( -N_{cr}/L \\): the force follows its chord as the joints move. A model without tension-only members does not enter this loop: it is solved once, by exactly the operations as before; a tension-only member that is never bowed or slack solves bit for bit as a truss.

**Why an active set on the cap, not the law inside the Newton loop.** Inside the Newton iterations, the capped law gives a tangent that jumps (from \\( EA/L \\) to 0) between iterations of one solution — the same primal–dual active-set step, with no more convergence guarantee — and mixes the set's changes with the P-Delta iterations. The cap as a constant force keeps each solve linear (first order) or a plain P-Delta solve, the switching outside, as it was; with \\( N_{cr} = 0 \\) it is the earlier tension-only method unchanged.

**Members left out together.** Switching every compressed member at once can leave out more than the structure can spare: both diagonals of an X panel are shortened by its legs under a heavy vertical load, yet as soon as both are out the panel sways and one of them is stretched. When the solve after members were left out fails (a mechanism, a stabilisation taking load, or buckling), it is repeated with only the more shortened half of those members left out (ranked by \\( (-N - N_{cr})/EA \\), the shortening strain beyond the load), and so on down to one; when even one member's leaving fails, that failure is the result — the structure needs that member beyond its buckling load.

**Joints held only by slack or bowed members.** A joint whose remaining members leave it free in some direction (the crossing of an X bracing whose compressed diagonal is out: the taut halves are collinear; a joint whose members all went out: every direction) is stabilised by the joint stabilisation applied to the model of the active members — the same rules as for a truss-only joint, so an axis is held exactly, a skew direction by the stiff spring. Nothing but the loads at the joint (and the bowed members' forces, which balance at a crossing) acts along such a direction (the active members' forces are normal to it), so the held force is that load component (zero without a load there), checked against the same limits. The held joint has no position of its own along those directions, so the stretch \\( \delta_m \\) of a left-out member is computed with its ends' held directions placed where the left-out members at them are in balance: the displacements \\( a_h \\) along the held directions \\( \mathbf d_h \\) minimise \\( \sum_m \frac{EA}{L}\\,\delta_m^2 \\) over the left-out members, \\( \delta_m = \mathbf e_m\cdot(\mathbf u_j - \mathbf u_i) + \sum_h \pm(\mathbf e_m\cdot\mathbf d_h)\\,a_h \\) — the limit of giving the left-out members a vanishing stiffness — solved per group of held directions linked by left-out members (a direction no left-out member reaches stays where it is). The two halves of a left-out diagonal through a held crossing then shorten alike, by half of the diagonal's shortening; with the joint left where the hold put it, one half could seem stretched and the set would flip back and forth. Giving the left-out members a small stiffness instead (springs) makes such joints nearly singular and gives meaningless displacements; the exact holds do not.

**Ties split where they are not one rope.** The tie rule assumes the halves carry one force — true at a crossing that nothing else holds along the tie. At a joint that a load, a spring or another member holds, the halves carry different forces: one half can be compressed (beyond its \\( N_{cr} \\)) while the tie end to end is not, and the tie, switched as a whole, can leave the joint nearly free and go round (in, its halves compressed; out, the nearly free joint moves far enough to stretch them). So, after the iterations:

- a set that goes round (a set solved before in the same phase, a **cycle**, or 100 solves) has its ties that change within the cycle **split** into their members, each switched by its own force and \\( N_{cr} \\) from then on, and the iterations run again;
- a settled solution with an active tie whose member is compressed beyond its own \\( N_{cr} \\) by more than \\( 10^{-6} \max |N| \\) has that tie split, and the iterations run again.

Each run splits at least one tie, so the runs end. Seen on the case that used to go round — a pair of diagonals in halves through a joint 1 mm off the line, the joint held otherwise only by a 1 kN/m spring, pulled along the frame: split, the joint is held by the two stretched halves (each \\( H/1.6 \\) by the method of joints) and the two shortened ones are slack (\\( I = 0 \\)) or bowed at their \\( N_{cr} \\), in 6 solves. The split is a refinement of the starting rule (one rope through a free crossing), not a guarantee: the member-by-member iterations are again primal–dual active-set steps, which for a strictly convex problem without the holds would be a semi-smooth Newton method (fast where it converges, no global guarantee); an energy-based update (a damped step or the complementary energy as a quadratic programme) would converge for this convex problem and is left for later (owner, O5).

**Last resort: kept active (approximate).** When single members still go round (no tie left to split), or after 1000 solves in all, the members going round are made **ordinary trusses** — active whatever their force — and the iterations run again on the others, accumulating if another set goes round (each round removes at least one tension-only member; with none left the model is solved once). The result is the exact solution of that modified model, no more: such a member may carry compression beyond its buckling load, which it cannot. The solution lists them (`Solution::kept_active`); the notes, the members' usage (*kept active (did not settle)*) and the structure usage (**approximate**, never a plain pass) say so. No model tested so far reaches it.

**Exit clauses.** One run of the iterations stops at a cycle or after 100 solves (then split, or the last resort, as above); 1000 solves in all end the splitting. Each solve has the exit clauses above, and the time limit (30 s by default) bounds all the solves of the iterations together: when it stops them, the error says so and how large the out-of-balance forces of the last solve were.

**Results.** A bowed member reports \\( N = -N_{cr} \\) (`MemberForces::bowed`); a slack one 0 (`slack`). The notes give `tension-only members: 3 of 140 slack, 12 bowed (4 solve(s)): …; bowed (carry their buckling load): D12 0.83 kN, …`.

## Member usage

Against the section's capacity times the load case's strength factor \\( \phi \\):

| Member | Usage |
|---|---|
| Truss in tension | \\( N / (\phi N_t) \\), \\( N_t \\) = the member's `capacity_tension`, else the allowable tension, else \\( A f_y \\) |
| Truss in compression | \\( \lvert N \rvert / (\phi N_c) \\), \\( N_c \\) = the member's `capacity_compression`, else \\( \min(\text{allowable or } A f_y,\\; \pi^2 E I_{min} / L_k^2) \\), \\( L_k \\) = the member's `lk`, else \\( kL \\) |
| Cable | tension as above; a slack cable uses 0 |
| Tension-only | tension as a truss; compression (at most \\( N_{cr} \\), the solve's cap) \\( \lvert N \rvert / (\phi N_c) \\), \\( N_c \\) = the member's `capacity_compression`, else the allowable compression, else \\( A f_y \\) — without the Euler load: buckling is in the solve, a bowed member carries its load and no more (marked "bowed: carries its buckling load … kN"); slack: 0 ("slack"); kept active (last resort): as a truss, marked "kept active (did not settle)" |
| Frame | \\( \lvert N \rvert/(\phi N_{t,c}) + M_y/(\phi M_{y,c}) + M_z/(\phi M_{z,c}) \\), largest end moments, \\( M_c \\) = allowable, else \\( W f_y \\) |

The Euler load with an effective length factor \\( k \\) is a simple stand-in: a design code's column curve (imperfections, residual stresses, slenderness of angle sections, eccentric connections) is not modelled — the user can give the code's allowable compression per section instead. A structure's FE usage is its worst member.

## Member wind

A member whose section comes from the section library has a profile; the wind on it (`Model::member_wind`) follows the cross-flow principle: with the unit wind direction \\( \hat{\mathbf w} \\), the member's axis \\( \hat{\mathbf x} \\) and the wind's component normal to it \\( \mathbf w_n = \hat{\mathbf w} - (\hat{\mathbf w}\cdot\hat{\mathbf x})\hat{\mathbf x} \\) (length \\( \sin\theta \\)), the force on the member is

\\[ \mathbf F = q\, c_d\, d\, L \sin\theta \; \mathbf w_n \qquad (|\mathbf F| = q\, c_d\, d\, L \sin^2\theta), \\]

\\( q \\) the wind pressure (times the load case's transverse factor), \\( c_d \\) the model's `drag` (default 1), \\( d \\) the profile's outline width across \\( \mathbf w_n \\) in the member's local y–z plane, \\( L \\) its length. Half of \\( \mathbf F \\) goes to each joint: statically equivalent for the rest of the structure (a cantilever's base moment is exactly \\( q d L^2/2 \\)), the member's own bending under the uniform load left out. No shielding between members, no solidity ratio, no code shape factors; the type's height-band wind (`[[wind.band]]`) is never put on the model, so the two are not added.

**On a height extension** a placed structure stands its `dz` higher. The member wind has no height in it (one pressure per weather case), so the force on each member is the same; the extension's own piece is part of the model and takes its member wind. The type's height bands (`[[wind.band]]`, kept out of the model) rise with the structure: a band from \( z_0 \) to \( z_1 \) above the ground of the structure as written acts at \( \tfrac12(z_0+z_1) + dz \), with the same force \( q\,c_d\,A \). OHL-Structure applies the same rule to a case's pressure on all members: on the extension chosen, the extension's own members take it too (\( p\,A_m \), half to each joint, \( A_m \) the projected area above).

## Pole check

A pole (`.ohlp`) is a shaft with a linear taper between the ground-line diameter \\( D_0 \\) and the top diameter \\( D_1 \\) over the height above ground \\( H = \ell - e \\) (length less embedment). A structure may describe its pole itself (`[pole]` in the structure file): its outline by height, round or polygonal, linear between the heights given; it is read into the same pole. At heights \\( h \\) from the ground line to the top (41 sections):

\\[ \mathbf M(h) = \sum_{z_i > h} (\mathbf r_i - h\hat{\mathbf z}) \times \mathbf F_i + \int_h^H p\\,c_d\\,D(z)\\,(z-h)\\,\mathrm dz\\;\hat{\mathbf z} \times \hat{\mathbf w} \\]

with the factored attachment loads \\( \mathbf F_i \\) at the type's attachment points \\( \mathbf r_i \\) (horizontal loads × height, vertical loads × their offset from the axis), the wind pressure \\( p \\) of the weather case times the transverse factor, the drag factor \\( c_d \\) and the wind direction \\( \hat{\mathbf w} \\) (across the line). The bending moment is \\( |(M_x, M_y)| \\); torsion is not checked.

**P-Delta amplification**: when the pole gives its modulus \\( E \\), a frame model of the pole above ground (12 tapered steps plus a node at every load, fixed at the ground line, solid or hollow, round or polygonal sections, its own weight when it gives a density) is solved first order and second order with the same loads (offsets as moments); the ratio of the ground-line moments multiplies the moment at every height — exact at the ground line, a simplification above.

**Capacity** at height \\( h \\): the table of the pole (linear between heights), else for wood \\( f_b \\) times the section modulus at \\( D(h) \\) (\\( \pi D(h)^3/32 \\) for a solid round pole) with the user's fibre stress \\( f_b \\), else the ground-line moment capacity. Usage: \\( \max_h M(h)/(\phi\\,M_c(h)) \\) — for a tapered wood pole the critical section can lie above the ground line.

**The same check in OHL-Structure** (its FE results and the structure report): the loads are the load table's point rows of the case at the structure's points as written (\( \mathbf r_i \) above the feet), the wind pressure \( p\,\hat{\mathbf w} \) the sum of the horizontal parts of the case's surface loads on all members (pressure × factor × direction; times the pole's \( c_d \) as above), \( \phi \) the case's strength factor. On a height extension the pole and its points rise together by `dz`: every \( \mathbf r_i - h\hat{\mathbf z} \) about the pole's own ground line is unchanged, and so is the check (the line's pole check takes the points as written too); the piece below the feet is not checked.

Not included: foundation (embedment) check, shear, deflection limits, guyed poles (use an `.ohlfe` model with cables), the wind on crossarms and equipment.
