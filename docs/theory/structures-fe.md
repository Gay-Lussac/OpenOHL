# Structure analysis: frames, trusses, guys and poles

Implemented in `crates/ohl-structures-fe`: a linear-elastic 3D finite-element model of a structure (lattice tower, frame, guyed mast, pole) under static loads, first order or with geometric non-linearity (P-Delta), and the member and pole checks built on it. It is a *base*: the formulation is standard matrix structural analysis; design-code specifics (column curves, connection checks, wind on the structure) are left to the data or to later work.

## Model and axes

Nodes are given in the **structure's axes**: x to the right of the line looking ahead, y ahead, z up, origin at the ground centre (the axes of the structure types, `.ohls`). Each node has six degrees of freedom \\( (u_x, u_y, u_z, \theta_x, \theta_y, \theta_z) \\); rotations exist only at nodes with a frame member, fixed degrees of freedom are removed, springs add to the diagonal.

Each member has **local axes**: \\( x \\) from node *i* to node *j*; a reference direction \\( \mathbf r \\) in the local x–y plane (default: global z, or global x for a vertical member) gives \\( \mathbf z = \mathbf x \times \mathbf r / |\cdot| \\), \\( \mathbf y = \mathbf z \times \mathbf x \\); a roll angle turns y and z about x. \\( I_z \\) is the bending in the local x–y plane, \\( I_y \\) in the x–z plane. An angle's legs given as two structure directions \\( \mathbf a, \mathbf b \\) (`orientation = "x+y-"`) set \\( \mathbf r = \mathbf a \\) when \\( \hat{\mathbf x}\cdot(\mathbf a_\perp \times \mathbf b_\perp) > 0 \\) (\\( \mathbf a_\perp \\): the part normal to the member), so that the cross-section's y leg points to \\( \mathbf a \\) and its z leg to the side of \\( \mathbf b \\); otherwise the pair is the mirror image of a right-handed cross-section: \\( \mathbf r = \mathbf b \\) for a cross-section with equal legs, refused for unequal ones. The element matrices are transformed with \\( \mathbf T = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf R, \mathbf R, \mathbf R, \mathbf R) \\), \\( \mathbf K = \mathbf T^T \mathbf k \mathbf T \\).

## Elements

- **Truss** (axial only): \\( \mathbf k = \frac{EA}{L}\\,\mathbf e\mathbf e^T \\) in the 3×3 blocks \\( \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf k & -\mathbf k \\\\ -\mathbf k & \mathbf k \end{bmatrix} \\).
- **Cable** (guys): a truss in tension only, with an initial force \\( T_0 \\) (pretension): \\( N = T_0 + \frac{EA}{L}\\,\mathbf e\cdot(\mathbf u_j - \mathbf u_i) \\). The pretension acts as a pair of nodal forces pulling the ends together; the force after the structure deflects differs from \\( T_0 \\) by the structure's give. A cable whose force would fall below zero is **slack**: it is removed and the solve repeated until the set of slack cables does not change.
- **Tension-only truss** (`tension`): a truss in tension and in compression up to its own elastic buckling load \\( N_{cr} \\); beyond it **bowed** — left out of the stiffness, carrying \\( N_{cr} \\) — and with \\( N_{cr} = 0 \\) (no second moment) slack, by the active-set iterations below. No pretension.
- **Tension-only member that bends** (`tension` with `bending = true`; a tower program's "tension-only beam"): a frame (below) while it is in the model — axial, bending and torsion, rotations at its joints, its self-weight as on frames — and, bowed or slack, a tension-only truss as above (its constant force, no stiffness, no bending, no rotations it alone gave its joints). Its bending is what holds a joint in the middle of such a member across it: an insulator hung from the middle of a rod between two crossarm chords, or the crossing of two diagonals whose halves are such members. As tension-only trusses that joint would be on a straight line of members, free across it.
- **Frame**: the 12×12 elastic stiffness of the 3D Euler–Bernoulli beam (axial \\( EA/L \\), torsion \\( GJ/L \\), bending \\( 12EI/L^3, 6EI/L^2, 4EI/L, 2EI/L \\) in both planes) — Przemieniecki, *Theory of Matrix Structural Analysis* (1968), ch. 5. Shear deformation is neglected.

**Simplification:** no shear deformation (Euler–Bernoulli beams). Short deep members deflect more than computed, so deflections and P-Delta effects are slightly low (unsafe, small for slender members). Exact: Timoshenko beams with the cross-sections' shear areas.

**Self-weight**: \\( w = f_d\\,\rho A g \\) per metre — \\( f_d \\) the member's dead-load factor (its own, else the model's `dead_load_factor`, default 1: bolts, plates) — times the load case's vertical factor. On frames as consistent nodal loads (\\( wL/2 \\) and \\( wL^2/12 \\) end moments, in local axes), on trusses and cables half at each end. The reported member end forces of frames subtract the fixed-end part, so they are the true end forces.

**Simplification:** the dead-load factor is 1 unless given: bolts, gusset plates and other connection steel are not in the self-weight (unsafe for the vertical loads by their share). Exact: a dead-load factor (or the parts' weight) from the design.

## Second order (P-Delta)

With `nonlinear = true` (the default), the tangent stiffness includes the **geometric stiffness** of each member's axial force \\( N \\) (tension positive):

- truss and cable: \\( \frac{N}{L}(\mathbf I - \mathbf e\mathbf e^T) \\) in the 3×3 blocks;
- frame: the consistent geometric stiffness of the cubic beam (terms \\( \frac{N}{L}\\{\tfrac65, \tfrac{L}{10}, \tfrac{2L^2}{15}, -\tfrac{L^2}{30}\\} \\) in both bending planes) — McGuire, Gallagher & Ziemian, *Matrix Structural Analysis* (2nd ed., 2000), ch. 9. The torsion–bending coupling terms are left out.

This captures both P-Δ (sway of the member chords) and P-δ (bowing within a frame member, refined by more elements). Displacements remain small (no corotational update of the geometry); the analysis is a *second-order elastic* analysis in the sense of McGuire et al., ch. 9.

**Simplification:** the torsion–bending coupling terms are left out of the geometric stiffness, and the geometry is not updated for large displacements. Flexural-torsional and lateral-torsional buckling of a frame member are therefore not found by the solve, and very flexible structures (a tall pole) are less exact (either way). Exact: the full 3D geometric stiffness with the Wagner terms, and a corotational large-displacement analysis.

**Newton iterations**: the out-of-balance forces \\( \mathbf r = \mathbf F - \mathbf K_T(N)\\,\mathbf u \\) are solved with the tangent \\( \mathbf K_T = \mathbf K_e + \mathbf K_g(N) \\) (the change of \\( N \\) with \\( \mathbf u \\) is not in the tangent), \\( \mathbf u \mathrel{+}= \mathbf K_T^{-1}\mathbf r \\), the axial forces and slack cables updated, until \\( |\mathbf r| \le 10^{-9} \\) of the larger of \\( |\mathbf F| \\) and the largest pretension, and the slack set is stable. Where the axial forces are statically determinate (a cantilever column) the second iteration is final; otherwise the iterations converge linearly with the ratio of the loads to the buckling load. A negative pivot in the factorisation means the tangent stiffness is no longer positive definite: the loads are above an **elastic buckling** load, reported as such.

**Exit clauses** (`ohl_core::budget`): 200 iterations and 30 s by default; when one stops the solve, the error says which and how large the out-of-balance forces still were.

## Solver

The stiffness is symmetric and banded once the nodes are numbered well: nodes are ordered by **reverse Cuthill–McKee** (George & Liu, *Computer Solution of Large Sparse Positive Definite Systems*, 1981) and the matrix is stored as a **skyline** (profile) and factorised \\( \mathbf L\mathbf D\mathbf L^T \\) in place (active-column method, Bathe, *Finite Element Procedures*, §8.2). The cost is about \\( n\bar b^2/2 \\) for \\( n \\) equations of mean column height \\( \bar b \\): a generated 1000-member lattice tower solves in a few milliseconds (release build). A zero pivot names the node and direction that are free to move (a mechanism).

Reactions are reported at every joint with a support or a spring: in each of its six directions, the forces of its members on it less its loads, \\( R = \mathbf f_{int} - \mathbf f_{ext} \\). At a fixed direction that is the support's reaction. A sprung direction moves, in balance with its spring, \\( \mathbf f_{int} + k u = \mathbf f_{ext} \\), so the same expression is the spring's force on the joint, \\( -k u \\) — translations (N/m) and rotations (N·m/rad) alike, first order and with P-Delta (there to the solve's residual). A direction both fixed and sprung does not move: the support takes everything and the spring nothing. So \\( \sum R + \sum \text{loads} = 0 \\) (the self-weight included) whether the structure stands on supports, springs or both; a joint held by springs only (a foundation on springs) has its reaction too. Benchmarks `structfe-009` (a beam on a vertical spring: the spring's force \\( Pa/L \\) by statics, its travel that over \\( k \\)) and `structfe-010` (a cantilever on a rotational spring: the base moment \\( PL \\), the tip \\( PL^3/3EI + PL^2/k_r \\)).

A **mechanism** is reported with the part that moves: after the zero pivot, the stiffness is assembled again and factorised with its zero pivots replaced by \\( 10^{-12} \\) of its largest diagonal entry; two steps of inverse iteration from a fixed start give a vector dominated by the zero-energy motions (they grow by the ratio of the stiffness to that stand-in, the others do not), and the joints translating more than 1/1000 of the largest translation (rotating, when none translates) are named.

## Joint stabilisation

Lattice towers modelled as trusses have joints with no stiffness of their own in some direction; the stiffness matrix is then singular. Following the practice of tower programs, which stabilise such joints, the solver holds those directions — only when a solve without them fails (a zero pivot, or a negative one: buckling), so that a model that solves as it is is solved by exactly the same operations and its results are unchanged bit for bit. The directions come from the geometry (`ohl_structures_fe::stabilise`):

- **Translations** of a joint without a frame member: the eigenvectors of \\( \mathbf S = \sum \mathbf e\mathbf e^T \\) over the unit directions \\( \mathbf e \\) of its truss and cable members and of its supported or sprung axes, with an eigenvalue below \\( 10^{-6} \\) of the largest (\\( \mathbf e^T\mathbf n \\) is the sine of a member's angle to the free plane, so this is about 0.06°): the normal of a plane of coplanar members, the plane normal to collinear ones. The joint's elastic stiffness along such an \\( \mathbf n \\) is \\( \sum \frac{EA}{L} (\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf n)^2 \approx 0 \\).
- **Rotations** about the common axis \\( \mathbf a \\) of a chain of collinear frame members, when no joint of the chain has a frame in another direction or a rotational support or spring with a component about \\( \mathbf a \\): a rigid rotation of the chain about its axis strains nothing (no twist between the ends, no bending, no translation). One joint of the chain (its first in node order) is held. Joints with truss members only have no rotational degrees of freedom at all.

**Held relative to its lines.** A free translation of a joint that lies on straight lines of members — two of its members pointing opposite ways within 0.6° (a member cut by the joint: a redundant's joint on a diagonal, a K joint on a horizontal, a plan bracing's middle joint) — is held relative to the ends of those lines, when the joint has no support or spring. A line's end is the first joint along it, through joints that continue it straight, that is not free itself, or is held relative to others already (rounds: a joint whose every line ends at such joints is held in the round, and counts as held in the next; so a chain of holds ends at held joints and never loops). With \\( a, b \\) a line's ends at distances \\( l_a, l_b \\) from the joint \\( J \\), the joint's place on the line is \\( \frac{l_b}{l_a + l_b}\mathbf u_a + \frac{l_a}{l_a + l_b}\mathbf u_b \\); with several lines, their mean: weights \\( w_e \\) over the ends, \\( \sum w_e = 1 \\). The hold is a spring on the joint's motion along \\( \mathbf n \\) less its lines',
\\[ \tfrac12 k \left(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_J - \textstyle\sum_e w_e\\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_e\right)^2, \qquad \mathbf K \mathrel{+}= k\\,\mathbf c\mathbf c^T, \\]
with \\( \mathbf c \\) holding \\( \mathbf n \\) at the joint and \\( -w_e\mathbf n \\) at each end, \\( k \\) the largest \\( EA/L \\) at the joint (the skyline's profile and ordering take the coupling of the joint and its ends). Its force \\( -k\\,\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf u \\) acts on the joint, and on each end its share \\( w_e \\) the other way: it stays in the structure. In first order it is the load along \\( \mathbf n \\) at the joint, passed to the line's ends as the straight member the joint cuts, pinned at its ends, carries a load across it (a beam's end reactions); the rest of the solution is that of the structure with that load at the ends. With P-Delta the joint follows its line, so the geometric stiffness of its members across \\( \mathbf n \\) pulls it by \\( \sum_k \frac{N_k}{L_k}(\mathbf u_J - \mathbf u_k)\cdot\mathbf n \\), which is zero for one line with the same force on both sides — the two halves of a cut member then have the whole member's geometric stiffness, \\( \frac{N}{l_a}(\mathbf u_J - \mathbf u_a) + \frac{N}{l_b}(\mathbf u_J - \mathbf u_b) = 0 \\) on the line — and small where the lines' points differ (an X crossing on a face that warps) or a member at the joint carries little. Held to the ground instead, the joint would stay put while its face sways, and its members' \\( N/L \\) times that sway would go to the ground.

**Held to the ground.** The other free translations (a joint on no straight line of members, or one whose lines end at joints free themselves — the joints of a plane truss, whose whole plane is free) and every rotation are held to the ground. A free direction along a structure axis (within \\( 1 - \cos < 10^{-9} \\)) is held **exactly**: its degree of freedom is removed, as a support would. A skew one gets a spring \\( k\\,\mathbf n\mathbf n^T \\) with \\( k \\) the largest \\( EA/L \\) (rotations: \\( GJ/L \\)) at the joint — for several free directions the sum is the projector onto the free space, whatever basis the eigen-solver returns. In first-order analysis nothing else acts along a free direction (the members' forces are normal to it), so the restraint's force is exactly the load component along it and the rest of the solution is that of the structure with the direction fixed; with P-Delta the geometric stiffness of tension members across the direction (\\( N/L \\)) couples it to its neighbours, and the restraint keeps it from moving, as a tower program's stabilisation does.

The force each stabilisation takes (the joint's out-of-balance along its direction; a relative hold's spring force) is reported, and a ground hold's subtracted from the reaction of a supported joint so that reactions are the supports' own (a relative hold's share at a supported end is in its reaction: the structure carries it). A stabilisation **held to the ground** that takes more than 1 % of the loads (the larger of their resultant and the largest load at a joint; a moment over the model's size) and more than a quarter of its joint's own load is refused as a mechanism: a load across a plane no member holds is not carried by the structure as a truss. Below that — the share of the self-weight across a battered face, sin(batter) of the joint's load — it is reported as a note. A hold **relative to its lines** is not refused, whatever it takes (relaxed for relative holds, owner 2026-10-19): its force goes to the lines' ends, so the structure carries it and the reactions balance the loads; above the same limits it is listed (`Solution::relative_over`) and flagged in the notes — `LARGE: 2 hold(s) relative to their lines take more than 1 % of the loads (carried by the lines' ends, not refused — check those joints): M z 1.00 kN, …`.

What is not a single joint's freedom (a rigid motion of a part of several joints: a missing support, a hinge chain) is not found by these rules and remains a zero pivot after the stabilisation: reported as a mechanism with the joints that move.

The threshold (an eigenvalue below \\( 10^{-6} \\) of the largest: members within about 0.06° of a plane or a line) assumes the geometry is as drawn. A model whose joints were rounded (an export with four significant digits: up to 5 mm at 10–99 m, about 0.1–0.3° over a metre-long member) can put a joint meant to be coplanar just above it: not free, but with a stiffness across the plane of \\( 10^{-6} \\)–\\( 10^{-4} \\) of the rest. It is not held, moves metres under its own weight in first order, and buckles at once with P-Delta (a negative pivot, "unstable"). That is wrong geometry, not a mechanism; an importer places such joints by their definitions (a joint at a fraction of the line between two others on that line) instead.

**Simplification:** a stabilisation is refused only when it takes more than 1 % of the loads *and* more than a quarter of its joint's own load. Below that, the force it takes does not go through the members (unsafe by that force, reported per joint). Exact: a model whose joints are all held by members.

**Simplification** (relaxed for relative holds, owner 2026-10-19): a hold relative to its lines carries any load across them, as if the member the joint cuts were a beam pinned at the lines' ends (its end shares \\( w_e \\)) — even where the real member is a truss or tension-only member with no bending to do so, or a joint loaded across a line of members only by rounding (an insulator hung from the middle of a tension-only rod imported without its bending). Large such holds are flagged, not refused: the load reaches the supports, but the members along the line are not checked for that bending. Exact: a model whose joints are all held by members (a member that bends, `frame` or `bending = true`, where a load acts across a line).

**Generated redundants and plan bracing.** The structure application's body generators make such joints on purpose ([format](../user-guide/structure-library.md#body-generators-groups-and-symmetry)): a redundant's joint lies in its face, a plan bracing's middle and centre joints in their level, so the direction across that plane is held. The patterns are chosen so that no rigid motion of several joints is left: every joint they add lies on a member and is braced to joints held already (each face, and each braced level, stays triangulated in its plane; the plan diamond has a cross to the centre, without which it would turn in plan). Every such joint lies on a member it cuts, whose ends are the legs' and the horizontals' joints (or, for a second level of redundants and the diamond's centre, joints held relative to them): all are held relative to their lines. On the 30 m test lattice tower of `apps/structure/ohl-structure-tests/tests/body_fe.rs` (five panels, 10 kN across, 5 kN along and 20 kN down at each of the four top corners, no self-weight) the held directions take, with P-Delta as in first order, below 5·10⁻⁴ of the loads (resultant and each one): at most 3.9·10⁻⁴ with two levels of redundants in X panels, the same in first order (joints rounded to the millimetre, a little off their face), 1.1·10⁻⁴ or less in the other patterns; held to the ground they took 0.2 to 1.1 % with P-Delta. The reactions alone balance the loads.

## Tension-only members: compression up to their buckling load

A tension-only member (`kind = "tension"`) stands for light bracing that buckles under a small compression. It follows a **capped law**: a truss in tension and in compression up to its own elastic buckling load, and no more:

\\[ N = \max\left(\frac{EA}{L}\\,\delta,\\; -N_{cr}\right), \qquad N_{cr} = \frac{\pi^2 E I_{min}}{L_k^2}, \\]

\\( \delta \\) its stretch, \\( L_k \\) its `lk`, else its `k` × its length — \\( k = 1 \\) by default: **pinned ends**, the member's own length between its joints (as for the compression check below). Shortened beyond \\( N_{cr} \\), the member is **bowed**: it carries \\( N_{cr} \\) and the further shortening costs nothing (an elastic strut after buckling keeps about its Euler load; the post-buckling rise and the bow's bending are left out). A member without a second moment (\\( I_{min} = 0 \\)) has \\( N_{cr} = 0 \\): **slack** in compression, as a rope — the earlier tension-only behaviour exactly. Not a sagging cable: a bowed member has no sag, no weight-dependent stiffness. This is the owner's decision of 2026-10-19 (ROADMAP, O5): it always solves, close to reality, where a member left out entirely could leave a part nearly free.

**Simplification:** a bowed member keeps carrying \\( N_{cr} \\), the load of a perfect elastic strut. A real member, with its crookedness and yielding, carries less once bowed, and the rest of the structure then carries more than computed (unsafe for the other members). Exact: a post-buckling law of the member (falling with its shortening), or a design code's buckling resistance as the cap.

The law is monotone (non-decreasing in \\( \delta \\)) and its energy convex — the elastic–perfectly-plastic law in compression, without unloading history. Solved as tower programs solve tension-only members, by a sequence of linear (or P-Delta) solves on a changing set of **active** members: a member out of the set (bowed, or slack) has no stiffness and carries the constant \\( N_{cr} \\) — a pair of forces pushing its ends apart, put with the loads like a cable's pretension, reported as its axial force. With \\( \mathcal S \\) the set left out, starting empty:

1. solve the model without the stiffness of the members of \\( \mathcal S \\), each carrying \\( -N_{cr} \\) (stabilised as above on the model of the active members);
2. \\( \mathcal S' = \\{ m \notin \mathcal S : N_m < -N_{cr,m} - \varepsilon \\} \cup \\{ m \in \mathcal S : \frac{EA}{L}\\,\delta_m \le -N_{cr,m} + \varepsilon \\} \\), with \\( \delta_m \\) the stretch of a left-out member (below) and \\( \varepsilon = 10^{-9} \max |N| \\) of the solve, so that round-off on a member at its load (or without force) does not switch it;
3. stop when \\( \mathcal S' = \mathcal S \\), else \\( \mathcal S \leftarrow \mathcal S' \\) and repeat.

A member compressed less than its \\( N_{cr} \\) stays active: a truss (a stocky tension-only member solves exactly as a truss). Tension-only members meeting at a joint on one line (within 0.06°) are switched as **one tie** — a diagonal in two halves through its crossing with the other is one rope: a half alone would hang from the crossing — by its stretch end to end over its flexibility, \\( \sum_m \delta_m / \sum_m \frac{L_m}{EA_m} \\) (\\( \delta_m = N_m L_m/EA_m \\) for an active member), in place of \\( N_m \\) and \\( \frac{EA}{L}\delta_m \\) above, against the least \\( N_{cr} \\) of its members (the force along the tie is one; at an unloaded crossing the bowed halves' forces then balance). All ties are switched at once. With **P-Delta** the set is found by first-order solves and then refined by P-Delta solves started from it, each converged (Newton) before the set is updated — outside the Newton loop: a compressed tension-only member's negative geometric stiffness \\( N/L \\) in the first solves is not real and could make the tangent indefinite, and an update inside the loop would change the matrix between iterations of one solution. A bowed member keeps the geometric stiffness of its constant force, \\( -N_{cr}/L \\): the force follows its chord as the joints move. A model without tension-only members does not enter this loop: it is solved once, by exactly the operations as before; a tension-only member that is never bowed or slack solves bit for bit as a truss.

**Why an active set on the cap, not the law inside the Newton loop.** Inside the Newton iterations, the capped law gives a tangent that jumps (from \\( EA/L \\) to 0) between iterations of one solution — the same primal–dual active-set step, with no more convergence guarantee — and mixes the set's changes with the P-Delta iterations. The cap as a constant force keeps each solve linear (first order) or a plain P-Delta solve, the switching outside, as it was; with \\( N_{cr} = 0 \\) it is the earlier tension-only method unchanged.

**Members left out together.** Switching every compressed member at once can leave out more than the structure can spare: both diagonals of an X panel are shortened by its legs under a heavy vertical load, yet as soon as both are out the panel sways and one of them is stretched. When the solve after members were left out fails (a mechanism, a stabilisation taking load, or buckling), it is repeated with only the more shortened half of those members left out (ranked by \\( (-N - N_{cr})/EA \\), the shortening strain beyond the load), and so on down to one; when even one member's leaving fails, that failure is the result — the structure needs that member beyond its buckling load.

**Joints held only by slack or bowed members.** A joint whose remaining members leave it free in some direction (the crossing of an X bracing whose compressed diagonal is out: the taut halves are collinear; a joint whose members all went out: every direction) is stabilised by the joint stabilisation applied to the model of the active members — the same rules as for a truss-only joint, so an axis is held exactly, a skew direction by the stiff spring. Nothing but the loads at the joint (and the bowed members' forces, which balance at a crossing) acts along such a direction (the active members' forces are normal to it), so the held force is that load component (zero without a load there), checked against the same limits. The held joint has no position of its own along those directions, so the stretch \\( \delta_m \\) of a left-out member is computed with its ends' held directions placed where the left-out members at them are in balance: the displacements \\( a_h \\) along the held directions \\( \mathbf d_h \\) minimise \\( \sum_m \frac{EA}{L}\\,\delta_m^2 \\) over the left-out members, \\( \delta_m = \mathbf e_m\cdot(\mathbf u_j - \mathbf u_i) + \sum_h \pm(\mathbf e_m\cdot\mathbf d_h)\\,a_h \\) — the limit of giving the left-out members a vanishing stiffness — solved per group of held directions linked by left-out members (a direction no left-out member reaches stays where it is). The two halves of a left-out diagonal through a held crossing then shorten alike, by half of the diagonal's shortening; with the joint left where the hold put it, one half could seem stretched and the set would flip back and forth. Giving the left-out members a small stiffness instead (springs) makes such joints nearly singular and gives meaningless displacements; the exact holds do not.

**Ties split where they are not one rope.** The tie rule assumes the halves carry one force — true at a crossing that nothing else holds along the tie. At a joint that a load, a spring or another member holds, the halves carry different forces: one half can be compressed (beyond its \\( N_{cr} \\)) while the tie end to end is not, and the tie, switched as a whole, can leave the joint nearly free and go round (in, its halves compressed; out, the nearly free joint moves far enough to stretch them). So, after the iterations:

- a set that goes round (a set solved before in the same phase, a **cycle**, or 100 solves) has its ties that change within the cycle **split** into their members, each switched by its own force and \\( N_{cr} \\) from then on, and the iterations run again;
- a settled solution with an active tie whose member is compressed beyond its own \\( N_{cr} \\) by more than \\( 10^{-6} \max |N| \\) has that tie split, and the iterations run again.

Each run splits at least one tie, so the runs end. Seen on the case that used to go round — a pair of diagonals in halves through a joint 1 mm off the line, the joint held otherwise only by a 1 kN/m spring, pulled along the frame: split, the joint is held by the two stretched halves (each \\( H/1.6 \\) by the method of joints) and the two shortened ones are slack (\\( I = 0 \\)) or bowed at their \\( N_{cr} \\), in 6 solves. The split is a refinement of the starting rule (one rope through a free crossing), not a guarantee: the member-by-member iterations are again primal–dual active-set steps, which for a strictly convex problem without the holds would be a semi-smooth Newton method (fast where it converges, no global guarantee); an energy-based update (a damped step or the complementary energy as a quadratic programme) would converge for this convex problem and is left for later (owner, O5).

**Last resort: kept active (approximate).** When single members still go round (no tie left to split), or after 1000 solves in all, the members going round are made **ordinary trusses** — active whatever their force — and the iterations run again on the others, accumulating if another set goes round (each round removes at least one tension-only member; with none left the model is solved once). The result is the exact solution of that modified model, no more: such a member may carry compression beyond its buckling load, which it cannot. The solution lists them (`Solution::kept_active`); the notes, the members' usage (*kept active (did not settle)*) and the structure usage (**approximate**, never a plain pass) say so. No model tested so far reaches it.

**Exit clauses.** One run of the iterations stops at a cycle or after 100 solves (then split, or the last resort, as above); 1000 solves in all end the splitting. Each solve has the exit clauses above, and the time limit (30 s by default) bounds all the solves of the iterations together: when it stops them, the error says so and how large the out-of-balance forces of the last solve were.

**Results.** A bowed member reports \\( N = -N_{cr} \\) (`MemberForces::bowed`); a slack one 0 (`slack`). The notes give `tension-only members: 3 of 140 slack, 12 bowed (4 solve(s)): …; bowed (carry their buckling load): D12 0.83 kN, …`.

## Attachment points, rigid links and split members

Strings hang from **attachment points**; each is linked to a **joint** of the structure (2026-10-21): a node, the place along a member where points sit, or — on a stick figure — its own joint. Several points may share a joint (their loads add up). Where the analysis puts a point's load:

- **On a node, no offset**: its force \\( \mathbf F \\) at the node, as before.
- **At an offset** \\( \mathbf r \\) from its joint (structure axes, from the joint to the point: a fitting, a link plate): the point and the joint are joined by a **rigid link**, so the load is moved to the joint as the same force plus its moment \\( \mathbf M = \mathbf r \times \mathbf F \\) (statics of a rigid body: exact for a link that does not deform). The moment is applied where a **frame member** (`kind = "frame"`, or a tension-only member that bends) meets the joint. Where only trusses, cables or tension-only members meet it, nothing in the model takes a moment there: it is **left out and said so** ("the moment of its offset … left out at joint J: only trusses meet it (make it a frame joint to carry it)"). **Simplification:** not on the safe side in general — the members near the joint miss the bending the eccentricity puts on them; exact: a frame joint there (a frame member to it), or the eccentricity modelled by members. The structure reports name every offset's moment (applied or left out).
- **Along a frame member** strictly between its ends (`member`, `at`): the analysis **splits the member there** — a node at the point, the member two pieces (more with several places) of its cross-section, kind, reference and roll — and the load (and an offset's moment) acts at that node, so the member bends under it as it does (the shares of before put the load at the member's ends: right for the reactions, not for the member's own moment under the point). The file keeps one member; the results are reported per member: its forces at its own two joints, the largest axial force of its pieces, its **usage the worst of its pieces'** (`Solution::pieces`). Each piece buckles over the **whole member's effective length** (its `lk`, else \\( k \times L \\) of the whole member), so the split changes no capacity: a split member is as stable as it was. Solving the split model is solving the same frame with a joint drawn there (bit for bit: unit test).
- **Along a truss member**: its force goes to the member's ends, \\( (1-a)\mathbf F \\) and \\( a\mathbf F \\) — statically equivalent for the member's axis, as a truss carries no load across it; an offset's moment only where frames meet both ends, else left out with the note above.
- **On no joint of the members** (a point at a position of its own on a structure with members): its load would reach nothing, so such a structure is not a full design (a missing item, "attachment point … on no joint of the members"): no finite-element check is run with a load left out.

A placed structure's own relink or offset wins over its type's; a point **unlinked** (its joint gone after a type changed, or unlinked by hand) keeps its place, and the structure's finite-element check is **refused** (usage infinite, "attachment point … is not linked to a joint of the type: its loads are refused until it is linked") — never run with that load left out. **Conservative:** refusing shows the structure as failing rather than passing with a load missing; exact: link the point (exact match, or the nearest attach joint offered at the offset left).

Benchmarks `structfe-013` (a cantilever crossarm, its tip carrying a point 0.5 m ahead: the mast takes \\( M_x = F e \\), the arm twists \\( F e L / GJ \\)) and `structfe-014` (a point along a fixed–fixed beam at a quarter: the beam split there, the reactions and deflection of Roark's intermediate load).

## Member usage

Against the cross-section's capacity times the load case's strength factor \\( \phi \\):

| Member | Usage |
|---|---|
| Truss in tension | \\( N / (\phi N_t) \\), \\( N_t \\) = the member's `capacity_tension`, else the allowable tension, else \\( A f_y \\) |
| Truss in compression | \\( \lvert N \rvert / (\phi N_c) \\), \\( N_c \\) = the member's `capacity_compression`, else \\( \min(\text{allowable or } A f_y,\\; \pi^2 E I_{min} / L_k^2) \\), \\( L_k \\) = the member's `lk`, else \\( kL \\) |
| Cable | tension as above; a slack cable uses 0 |
| Tension-only | tension as a truss (one that bends, while in the model: as a frame, its moments too); compression (at most \\( N_{cr} \\), the solve's cap) \\( \lvert N \rvert / (\phi N_c) \\), \\( N_c \\) = the member's `capacity_compression`, else the allowable compression, else \\( A f_y \\) — without the Euler load: buckling is in the solve, a bowed member carries its load and no more (marked "bowed: carries its buckling load … kN"); slack: 0 ("slack"); kept active (last resort): as a truss, marked "kept active (did not settle)" |
| Frame | \\( \lvert N \rvert/(\phi N_{t,c}) + M_y/(\phi M_{y,c}) + M_z/(\phi M_{z,c}) \\), largest end moments, \\( M_c \\) = allowable, else \\( W f_y \\) |
| Not checked (`check = false`) | none: "not checked (modelling member)" — a member that is part of the model only (a tower program's *fictitious* member: an insulator's support rod, a member that only carries a load into the structure); its forces are solved and reported |

The Euler load with an effective length factor \\( k \\) is a simple stand-in: a design code's column curve (imperfections, residual stresses, slenderness of angle cross-sections, eccentric connections) is not modelled — the user can give the code's allowable compression per cross-section instead. A structure's FE usage is its worst member.

**Simplification:** the compression capacity \\( \min(A f_y, \pi^2 E I_{min}/L_k^2) \\) without a column curve. It is **not on the safe side**: at intermediate slenderness a real member buckles well below both terms (imperfections, residual stresses, eccentric connections). Exact: the design code's buckling resistance, given as the cross-section's allowable compression or the member's `capacity_compression`.

**Conservative / Simplification:** the buckling length \\( L_k = k L \\) with \\( k = 1 \\) by default: pinned ends, over the member's own length between its joints. On the safe side where the joints hold the member in every direction (end restraint would give \\( k < 1 \\)). Not on the safe side where a joint does not hold it sideways: a diagonal drawn in two halves through an unbraced crossing buckles out of its plane over its whole length, not each half (open point O12, owner: depends on the joint type, OK for now). Exact: the buckling length per direction from the bracing pattern and the joints (give `lk` or `k`).

**Simplification:** a tension-only member in compression is checked against its compression capacity without the Euler term (its force is capped at \\( N_{cr} \\) by the solve). A bowed member therefore shows the usage of its cross-section, not that it has buckled; with the cap of a perfect strut (above) its real load-bearing is overstated. Decided with the owner as open point O12: OK for now, to be discussed again. Exact: a design code's rule for slender bracing in compression.

**Simplification:** frames are checked with the largest end moments and a linear interaction. A moment larger inside the member (from its own weight or member wind, which this check does not see between the joints) is missed (unsafe for long members under their own load). Exact: the moment along the member, and the code's interaction with its amplification.

**Simplification:** a member kept active as the last resort (above) is a truss whatever its force: it may carry compression beyond its buckling load. The usage is marked *approximate* and never a pass. Exact: a settled active set.

## Member wind

A member whose cross-section comes from the cross-section library has a profile; the wind on it (`Model::member_wind`) follows the cross-flow principle: with the unit wind direction \\( \hat{\mathbf w} \\), the member's axis \\( \hat{\mathbf x} \\) and the wind's component normal to it \\( \mathbf w_n = \hat{\mathbf w} - (\hat{\mathbf w}\cdot\hat{\mathbf x})\hat{\mathbf x} \\) (length \\( \sin\theta \\)), the force on the member is

\\[ \mathbf F = q\, c_d\, d\, L \sin\theta \; \mathbf w_n \qquad (|\mathbf F| = q\, c_d\, d\, L \sin^2\theta), \\]

\\( q \\) the structures' wind pressure of the load case times its factor (below; by default the weather case's pressure times the transverse factor), \\( c_d \\) the model's `drag` (default 1), \\( d \\) the profile's outline width across \\( \mathbf w_n \\) in the member's local y–z plane, \\( L \\) its length. Half of \\( \mathbf F \\) goes to each joint: statically equivalent for the rest of the structure (a cantilever's base moment is exactly \\( q d L^2/2 \\)), the member's own bending under the uniform load left out. The type's height-band wind (`[[wind.band]]`) is never put on the model, so the two are not added.

**Simplification:** the member's own bending under its wind is left out (half the force to each joint). The rest of the structure is exact; the member itself is unsafe by its own bending (up to \\( F L/8 \\) for a pinned member, \\( F \\) its wind force; matters for long frame members). Exact: the load as consistent nodal loads with its fixed-end moments.

**Simplification:** without wind factors, the generic drag factor 1 on every profile. A design code's drag factor for a profile or a lattice face can be higher, so the wind can be low (unsafe) — give the factors (below). Exact: the code's drag factors.

Without wind factors in the cross-section library that is all: one drag factor, no shielding. With them (a `[wind]` table, from a design-code plugin or the user) \\( c_d \\) becomes the drag of the profile's shape or of its lattice face by its solidity ratio, and the members of a face behind another take a shielding factor: see [Wind on lattice structures](structure-wind.md) for the faces, their solidity ratios and which members are shielded.

**On a extension** a placed structure stands its `dz` higher. The member wind has no height in it (one pressure per weather case), so the force on each member is the same; the extension's own piece is part of the model and takes its member wind. The type's height bands (`[[wind.band]]`, kept out of the model) rise with the structure: a band from \( z_0 \) to \( z_1 \) above the ground of the structure as written acts at \( \tfrac12(z_0+z_1) + dz \), with the same force \( q\,c_d\,A \). OHL-Structure applies the same rule to a case's pressure on all members: on the extension chosen, the extension's own members take it too (\( p\,A_m \), half to each joint, \( A_m \) the projected area above).

## The structures' wind of a load case

The wind on a structure's own body is \\( q = p_s\\, \\gamma_s \\): \\( p_s \\) the load case's `[case.structure]` `wind_pressure`, else its weather case's `wind_pressure`; \\( \\gamma_s \\) its `wind_factor`, else its `transverse` factor (`ohl_loads::LoadCase::structure_wind`). The wires and strings always take the weather case's pressure and the transverse factor, so a design code treating the wind on structures apart from the wind on the conductors writes the structures' values there (by hand, or a design-code plugin). One rule for every use: the height bands (\\( p_s\\,c_d\\,A \\) unfactored, \\( \\times\\gamma_s \\) factored), the member wind above, the pole's \\( p \\) below, and the line cases in OHL-Structure (sent in the line values as \\( p_s \\), \\( \\gamma_s \\) and the side; there a surface load of \\( q \\) on every member, and the pole's wind). Without the table, \\( q \\) is the weather case's pressure times the transverse factor, as before. Example (invented): a wind case of 600 Pa with transverse 1.4 and `[case.structure]` 900 Pa × 1.2 gives the wires 600 × 1.4 = 840 Pa and the structures 900 × 1.2 = 1 080 Pa; a band of 10 m² with drag 3 carries 900 × 30 = 27 kN, factored 32.4 kN (validation cases `loadwind-001` and `-002`, `benchmarks/weather/load_case_wind.toml`; the line's checks and OHL-Structure's pole check of the same case agree to the last digit, `apps/structure/ohl-structure-tests/tests/structure_wind.rs`).

The pressure is one rule; how it becomes member forces is the member wind's: the line's FE check puts \\( q \\) on the members with the drag and shielding factors of the cross-section library's `[wind]` table (above; else the generic drag), while OHL-Structure's surface loads (its recipes' pressures and the line cases' wind on the body) take \\( q \\) × the projected area along the wind, with no drag and no shielding. So the same case can load the members of a lattice differently in the two; the factors on OHL-Structure's surface loads are still open (U5).

## Pole check

A pole (`.ohlp`) is a shaft with a linear taper between the ground-line diameter \\( D_0 \\) and the top diameter \\( D_1 \\) over the height above ground \\( H = \ell - e \\) (length less embedment). A structure may describe its pole itself (`[pole]` in the structure file): its outline by height, round or polygonal, linear between the heights given; it is read into the same pole. At heights \\( h \\) from the ground line to the top (41 cross-sections):

\\[ \mathbf M(h) = \sum_{z_i > h} (\mathbf r_i - h\hat{\mathbf z}) \times \mathbf F_i + \int_h^H p\\,c_d\\,D(z)\\,(z-h)\\,\mathrm dz\\;\hat{\mathbf z} \times \hat{\mathbf w} \\]

with the factored attachment loads \\( \mathbf F_i \\) at the type's attachment points \\( \mathbf r_i \\) (horizontal loads × height, vertical loads × their offset from the axis), the structures' wind pressure of the load case \\( p = p_s\\,\\gamma_s \\) (above; by default the weather case's times the transverse factor), the drag factor \\( c_d \\) and the wind direction \\( \hat{\mathbf w} \\) (across the line). The bending moment is \\( |(M_x, M_y)| \\); torsion is not checked.

**P-Delta amplification**: when the pole gives its modulus \\( E \\), a frame model of the pole above ground (12 tapered steps plus a node at every load, fixed at the ground line, solid or hollow, round or polygonal cross-sections, its own weight when it gives a density) is solved first order and second order with the same loads (offsets as moments); the ratio of the ground-line moments multiplies the moment at every height — exact at the ground line, a simplification above.

**Simplification:** that ratio above the ground line. The second-order share of the moment differs with the height (either way), so for a critical section above the ground line the amplified moment is approximate. Exact: the second-order moment at every height from the frame model.

**Simplification:** without a modulus \\( E \\) there is no P-Delta amplification at all: the moments are first order (unsafe for a slender pole under vertical load). Exact: the pole's modulus given.

**Capacity** at height \\( h \\): the table of the pole (linear between heights), else for wood \\( f_b \\) times the section modulus at \\( D(h) \\) (\\( \pi D(h)^3/32 \\) for a solid round pole) with the user's fibre stress \\( f_b \\), else the ground-line moment capacity. Usage: \\( \max_h M(h)/(\phi\\,M_c(h)) \\) — for a tapered wood pole the critical cross-section can lie above the ground line.

**The same check in OHL-Structure** (its FE results and the structure report): the loads are the load table's point rows of the case at the structure's points as written (\( \mathbf r_i \) above the feet), the wind pressure \( p\,\hat{\mathbf w} \) the sum of the horizontal parts of the case's surface loads on all members (pressure × factor × direction; times the pole's \( c_d \) as above) — for a line case, the line's \( p_s\,\gamma_s \) (the same pole check as the line's), \( \phi \) the case's strength factor. On a extension the pole and its points rise together by `dz`: every \( \mathbf r_i - h\hat{\mathbf z} \) about the pole's own ground line is unchanged, and so is the check (the line's pole check takes the points as written too); the piece below the feet is not checked.

Not included: foundation (embedment) check, shear, deflection limits, guyed poles (use an `.ohlfe` model with cables), the wind on crossarms and equipment.

**Simplification:** what is not included is not on the safe side: the wind on crossarms and equipment is missing from the moment (unsafe), and torsion, shear and the foundation are not checked. Exact: their loads at the attachment points, and the checks of the design code.
